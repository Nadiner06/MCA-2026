## Week 1
The Theme I picked for my Portfolio is the song Married Life by Michael Giacchino.

###### Challenges With Working With Music
1. Access - getting access to certain music might be harder due to licensing rights, and being able to use music in projects raises issues a lot due to this. If wanting to use music within projects and it not being in the public domain you would either need to buy the rights or ask for permission and this is not always doable.
2. Another access issue that you may find with digital music is now a lot of platforms require an account or subscription in to listen. The issue with subscription services rises here as well as you are paying for something that you don't physically own but instead paying for access to be able to listen or listen without interruption.
3. Curating your own music on platforms like Spotify it uses playlists and algorithmic recommendations to give to people to listen this means it might be influenced by the platform and not by your own choices. Or it could be solely focusing on the songs that you have liked and click on continuously and feed you these songs over and over.
4. Another issue that could occur is that different versions could be made and distributed on different channels and platforms this could either result in issues of copyright or could result in piracy or not giving full credit to the makers of songs.

## Week 2
My chosen piece is Married Life by Michael Giacchino, I accessed it through MuseScore and transcribed it from scratch.

My Score: [Married Life](tasks/Married_life_2.mscz)

**The Good**  
The areas that worked well were the note selection; I think this was very easy to get used to and works well, especially when choosing different pitches. It was easy to follow and set up the original layout. I was also able to select the instrument that I wanted to use so that it sounded the same way as the original; this allowed me to correct the timbre of the piece, which I identified as piano. I was also able to add the time signature, which was a 3/4 time signature. It also allowed me to pick the key signature, which was F major. This set up my piece for me; it also helped with identifying what they meant and learning the key piece within a score and deepening my knowledge of the components of music. 

**The Challenges**  
One thing I did find challenging and struggled to do was display the bars like the original version that I was working with, there wasn't enough space to display all the bars on the first line, and so it had to go onto a new line, so there were only 6 bars on top and then the rest followed. Through multiple tries, I just couldn’t quite get the bars to fit in the top one, which was frustrating, but when played back, it never really interfered with the way it sounded compared to the original or the audio version. After looking through MuseScore, I was able to lock the number of bars per system, which was found in the format tab.

**The Changes**   
One thing I decided to leave out was a small note on bar 10 that sat above the original note. After a little searching, I think it is called a crush note, a small note with a slash through its stem. I played around with MuseScore and its different functions however, I was never able to complete this note. When playing the piece that I had created, I never noticed any major difference in the sound of my piece compared to the original, so I felt that it wasn't necessary to add this in.
